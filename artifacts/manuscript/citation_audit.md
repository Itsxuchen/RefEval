## Selected keys and supported uses

### Data sources and the motivating practice

1. **`peng2026ruverbench` — Peng et al. (2026), _Can LLM-as-a-Judge Reliably Verify Rubrics in Agentic Scenarios?_** [Record and author list, v2, 2 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29920v2); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29920v2). The record reports EMNLP 2026 acceptance; the pinned reading copy remains the preprint. Section 3 and Appendix B support the released criterion-level verification setting, upstream filtering and two-group annotation/adjudication procedure. The reported 90.44% agreement and κ = 0.808 are criterion-level agreement before adjudication, not task-level reliability. The current paper constructs its all-retained-criteria-pass target; RuVerBench does not supply this as its headline metric. Do not claim that task linkage is absent from its public data.

2. **`yang2026judgmentbench` — Yang et al. (2026), _JudgmentBench: Comparing Rubric and Preference Evaluation for Quality Assessment_.** [Record, v3, 4 June 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25240v3); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25240v3). The experiment uses 30 legal tasks and compares rubric and preference judgments over constructed quality levels. Its 1,539 rubric annotations are not 1,539 independent task designs. Table 4 supports the reported average rank-correlation contrast, but neither that contrast nor the source establishes our strict-AND target as occupational ground truth. Cite the paper for provenance and measurement motivation; define our transformed target separately.

3. **`vidgen2026apex` — Vidgen et al. (2026), _APEX-Agents_.** [Record, v3, 23 February 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14242v3); [full text, scoring and judge-validation sections](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14242v3). The native task-success metric requires all criteria to pass. The judge validation in §4.2 uses 60 tasks, 249 criteria and three model outputs per criterion: 747 criterion-output judgments, with TP 208, FN 4, FP 7 and TN 528, yielding 98.5% agreement. These are not 747 independently sampled tasks. Use this as a concrete motivation for studying what criterion summaries imply about task conclusions; do not infer actual APEX task errors from RuVerBench. This pinned paper is distinct from the September 2026 APEX-Agents 1.1 release.

### Boolean evaluation and short-circuiting

4. **`unluyurt2004sequential` — Tonguç Ünlüyurt (2004), _Sequential testing of complex systems: a review_.** Discrete Applied Mathematics 142(1–3), 189–205; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2002.08.001), [author manuscript](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/reucaar/surveyeval.pdf). The series/parallel-system discussion explains sequential Boolean testing and, for a series system with independent component states and known probabilities/costs, ordering by cost divided by failure probability. Cite this to acknowledge classical short-circuit evaluation and cost-aware ordering. A judge-predicted-failure-first heuristic is related to this rule but is not the same algorithm with known calibrated failure probabilities.

5. **`gkenosis2022sbfe` — Dimitrios Gkenosis, Nathaniel Grammel, Lisa Hellerstein and Devorah Kletenik (2022), _The Stochastic Boolean Function Evaluation Problem for Symmetric Boolean Functions_.** Discrete Applied Mathematics 309, 269–277; [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166218X21004790), DOI 10.1016/j.dam.2021.12.001; [reading copy v3, 4 January 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.08793v3). The problem definition uses independent bits with known probabilities and positive query costs; adaptive testing ends once the function value is determined. The algorithms have stated approximation guarantees for this model. Evaluation and verification need not have the same minimum expected cost. Do not import these guarantees into an empirical heuristic whose reference distribution is unknown.

6. **`ghuge2026certification` — Rohan Ghuge, Jai Moondra and Mohit Singh (2026), _Stochastic Function Certification with Correlations_.** [Record, v1, 3 April 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02611v1); [full text, problem definition and results](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02611v1). The certification problem permits correlation, but its distribution is supported on satisfying realizations; it is a one-sided witness-acquisition problem. Approximation results depend on the stated function and distribution models. Cite it for the distinction between learning a function value and acquiring a certificate. It does not prove a generic optimality result for our two-sided, budget-limited reference replay.

7. **`gou2025mind2web2` — Gou et al. (2025), _Mind2Web 2: Evaluating Agentic Search with Agent-as-a-Judge_.** [Record and author list, v2, 3 July 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21506v2); [full text, Appendix D.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21506v2). The agent judge can short-circuit critical and sequential criteria to reduce judge execution cost; this optimization is disabled for the all-node meta-evaluation. This is a direct agent-evaluation precedent for dependency-aware criterion execution. Its budget unit is judge execution, not acquisition of fixed reference bits, and its short circuit should not be described as the same intervention as eager versus certificate-gated replacement.

### The closest query, update and selection comparisons

8. **`krivosheev2018screening` — Evgeny Krivosheev, Fabio Casati and Boualem Benatallah (2018), _Crowd-based Multi-Predicate Screening of Papers in Literature Reviews_.** WWW 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3178876.3186036), [reading copy v1, 21 March 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.09814v1). The Shortest Run framework allocates crowd questions to item–filter pairs, updates uncertain inclusion decisions and balances error loss against expert cost. This is a close precedent for conjunction-like inclusion and selective verification. Its stopping decisions use probabilistic crowd models; they are not exact reference-bit certificates. Report loss weights as experimental choices rather than universal theorem assumptions.

9. **`krivosheev2018hybrid` — Evgeny Krivosheev, Fabio Casati, Marcos Baez and Boualem Benatallah (2018), _Combining Crowd and Machines for Multi-predicate Item Screening_.** PACM HCI 2(CSCW), Article 97; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3274366), [published paper](https://marcosbaez.com/assets/pdf/krivosheev2018combining.pdf). The hybrid screening method uses machine evidence and crowd answers to update item–filter posteriors and direct further queries, with expert fallback. Machine correlation is considered. Cite it when motivating the need to compare against existing multi-predicate human–machine screening, not only generic active learning. Its 2019 arXiv posting does not change the 2018 publication year.

10. **`krivosheev2020active` — Evgeny Krivosheev, Burcu Sayin, Alessandro Bozzon and Zoltán Szlávik (2020), _Active Learning from Crowd in Document Screening_.** Crowd Science workshop co-located with NeurIPS 2020, CEUR volume 2736, 19–25; [paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2736/paper4.pdf). Selection uses uncertainty about one predicate together with the chance that other predicates pass; crowd labels retrain a classifier. The experiment evaluates screening F1 under different label scenarios. This already makes selection sensitive to the downstream multi-predicate objective. It is a workshop paper, not a NeurIPS main-track paper, and supplies neither design-unbiased prevalence inference nor logical per-item certification.

11. **`krishnan2016activeclean` — Sanjay Krishnan, Jiannan Wang, Eugene Wu, Michael J. Franklin and Ken Goldberg (2016), _ActiveClean: Interactive Data Cleaning for Statistical Modeling_.** PVLDB 9(12), 948–959; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.14778/2994509.2994514), [paper](https://activeclean.github.io/files/activeclean-vldb16.pdf). Section 4 separates which records to clean from how to update a statistical model. Known sampling probabilities and support conditions enable unbiased gradient estimates; convex-loss assumptions support convergence analysis. Cite it as prior selection/update separation. The guarantee is not monotonic reduction of finite-population task-verdict errors after every cleaned record.

12. **`schreiber2026queryguided` — Ran Schreiber and Yael Amsterdamer (2026), _Query-Guided Analysis and Mitigation of Data Verification Errors_.** ICDE 2026; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDE65706.2026.00151), [extended reading copy v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08612v2). The paper asks which verification errors threaten a query result and which cells merit further verification. Its labeling-likelihood model uses independent verification errors; the maximal error scenario is a highest-likelihood label configuration that changes the answer, not a posterior error probability. Risk mitigation calculations hold current labels fixed while reducing error probabilities, whereas real re-verification can change labels. Hardness results apply to the stated general query classes; do not turn them into a hardness claim about simple AND certification.

13. **`reitich2026correction` — Fernando Reitich (2026), _Correction and Corruption: A Two-Rate View of Error Flow in LLM Protocols_.** [Record, v3, 10 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18245v3); [full text, §§2 and 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18245v3). Equation 3 gives an exact correction-minus-corruption accounting identity. Section 3.4 also evaluates selective application using fitted rates; application cost is not modeled. Cite both facts: this is more than an abstract analogy. Our added content must be the AND query-completion mechanism, fixed-reference budget accounting and observed stability, not first discovery of error-flow decomposition or selective use of such a decomposition.

14. **`jung2025trust` — Jaehun Jung, Faeze Brahman and Yejin Choi (2025), _Trust or Escalate: LLM Judges with Provable Guarantees for Human Agreement_.** [Formal ICLR 2025 record](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/08dabd5345b37fffcbe335bd578b15a0-Abstract-Conference.html); [paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/08dabd5345b37fffcbe335bd578b15a0-Paper-Conference.pdf). Sections 2–2.3 calibrate confidence thresholds for pairwise preference judgments and escalation/abstention. The guarantee concerns agreement in the accepted population, with high probability over calibration data. Section 3.4 requires i.i.d. calibration data for the stated guarantee; distribution-shift experiments do not remove that assumption. It does not certify every accepted preference, nor does it supply an AND certificate from observed criteria. Cite the formal 2025 venue rather than only its 2024 preprint.

### Finite-label inference and competing testing objectives

15. **`horvitz1952generalization` — D. G. Horvitz and D. J. Thompson (1952), _A Generalization of Sampling Without Replacement From a Finite Universe_.** JASA 47(260), 663–685; [publisher record and abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01621459.1952.10483446), DOI 10.1080/01621459.1952.10483446; [original scan](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~brian/905-2008/papers/Horvitz-Thompson-1952-jasa.pdf). Metadata and the original abstract were checked; the scanned full text was not text-readable through this audit interface. This supports the historical attribution for unequal-probability finite-population sampling. Keep the manuscript's elementary inverse-inclusion-probability argument self-contained. Do not imply this source supplies our particular hypergeometric-tail implementation or all of its numerical edge cases. The publisher's 2012 online-posting date is not the publication year.

16. **`chaganty2018price` — Arun Tejasvi Chaganty, Stephen Mussmann and Percy Liang (2018), _The price of debiasing automatic metrics in natural language evaluation_.** ACL 2018, 643–653; [record](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1060/), [paper](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1060.pdf), DOI 10.18653/v1/P18-1060. Section 3 uses control variates to combine automatic metrics with sampled noisy human evaluations. Its target is average expected human judgment, and its efficiency decomposition explicitly includes annotator variance. Theorem 3.1 is a minimax variance result under its specified model, not a universal best-budget protocol theorem. The bibliography follows the correctly spelled PDF title; the Anthology page title has a typo.

17. **`angelopoulos2023ppi` — Anastasios N. Angelopoulos, Stephen Bates, Clara Fannjiang, Michael I. Jordan and Tijana Zrnic (2023), _Prediction-Powered Inference_.** Science 382(6671), 669–674; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi6000), [reading copy v4, 9 November 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.09633v4). Prediction-powered inference combines predictions with a labeled rectifier to estimate population parameters and construct valid intervals under the stated design. The paper contains both population and finite-population treatments; do not say PPI universally requires only one particular i.i.d. setup. It does not convert its aggregate parameter interval into a certificate for each unit or guarantee a variance reduction for arbitrary predictions.

18. **`fisch2024stratppi` — Adam Fisch, Joshua Maynez, R. Alex Hofer, Bhuwan Dhingra, Amir Globerson and William W. Cohen (2024), _Stratified Prediction-Powered Inference for Effective Hybrid Evaluation of Language Models_.** NeurIPS 37; [formal record](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/c9fcd02e6445c7dfbad6986abee53d0d-Abstract-Conference.html), [paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c9fcd02e6445c7dfbad6986abee53d0d-Paper-Conference.pdf). Sections 3–4 provide stratified prediction-powered estimation and allocation to improve statistical efficiency. The main normal interval derivation is asymptotic under its sampling/regularity conditions. It is not the same finite exact interval construction as our conditional hypergeometric method. The PDF uses a shorter title; the bibliography appropriately preserves the formal proceedings title. Our charged-pilot stratified control is not a reimplementation of all StratPPI variants.

19. **`kim2026humanreviews` — Jane Paik Kim (2026), _Augmenting Human Evaluation with LLM Judges: How Many Human Reviews Do You Need?_** [Record, v1, 8 May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16354v1); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16354v1), DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2605.16354. Sections 2–3 assume LLM ratings on the evaluation pool and human ratings on a subset with known observation probabilities. A conditional prediction of human ratings and weighted residuals support estimation; sample-size and stratum-allocation planning use asymptotic variance and pilot-estimated predictive R². R² is not necessarily the correlation of raw LLM ratings with humans. Section 4 warns that optimistic pilots can underallocate and treats human labels as a gold standard. This is a single-author paper; use “Kim,” not “Kim et al.”

20. **`nguyen2018activetesting` — Phuc Nguyen, Deva Ramanan and Charless Fowlkes (2018), _Active Testing: An Efficient and Robust Framework for Estimating Accuracy_.** ICML, PMLR 80, 3759–3768; [record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/nguyen18d.html), [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/nguyen18d/nguyen18d.pdf). Sections 3–4 choose a subset of labels to vet and estimate accuracy using a learned posterior over unvetted labels. The posterior modeling assumptions matter. Cite it for active accuracy estimation and selection; do not attribute design-unbiasedness under arbitrary misspecified posteriors to this method.

21. **`guerriero2021deepest` — Antonio Guerriero, Roberto Pietrantuono and Stefano Russo (2021), _Operation is the Hardest Teacher: Estimating DNN Accuracy Looking for Mispredictions_.** ICSE 2021, 348–358; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE43902.2021.00042), [reading copy v1, 8 February 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.04287v1). Section III's DeepEST combines failure-oriented adaptive sampling with known selection probabilities and weighted estimation. This already connects error discovery with accuracy estimation under a single test budget. Its guarantee depends on the sampling design; it does not provide our task-level AND certificates or eager/gated update comparison. Accordingly, “jointly considering multiple audit outputs” alone is insufficient novelty.

22. **`zhang2026testselection` — Jingyu Zhang, Fan Wang, Jacky Keung, Yihan Liao, Yan Xiao and Lei Ma (2026), _Empirical Insights of Test Selection Metrics under Multiple Testing Objectives and Distribution Shifts_.** PACM Software Engineering 3(FSE), Article FSE058; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3797086), [reading copy v1, 25 April 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23342v1). Section 3.3 and experiments compare test selection for fault discovery, performance estimation and retraining across budgets and shifts. Cite it for objective-dependent value of selected labels. Our distinction must be the particular conjunction/repair/certification estimands and fixed-query comparison, not the general observation that testing objectives can favor different selectors.

23. **`ao2026audits` — Ruicheng Ao, Hongyu Chen, Siyang Gao, Hanwei Li and David Simchi-Levi (2026), _Best Arm Identification with LLM Judges and Limited Human Audits_.** [Record, v1, 29 January 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21471v1); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21471v1). The method chooses costly high-fidelity audits with positive audit propensities, combines them with lower-fidelity judge information and uses confidence-sequence machinery for best-arm identification. Its experimental section is synthetic. Cite it for a decision-level use of limited audits; do not group it under “only point estimation.” Best-arm decisions and individual AND certificates are different targets. The existing note correctly records that the abstract title omits the final word “Audits,” which appears in the paper text.

### Reference uncertainty, reporting and information sufficiency

24. **`elangovan2025uncertainty` — Aparna Elangovan, Lei Xu, Jongwoo Ko, Mahsa Elyasi, Ling Liu, Sravan Babu Bodapati and Dan Roth (2025), _Beyond correlation: The impact of human uncertainty in measuring the effectiveness of automatic evaluation and LLM-as-a-judge_.** [Formal ICLR 2025 record](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/8798321486948322be2b4d658744ba72-Abstract-Conference.html); [full-text reading copy arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03775v3). Sections 3–4 demonstrate how aggregating over different levels of human disagreement can obscure human–machine differences, and recommend uncertainty-stratified reporting and distributional diagnostics. Cite this for treating repeated human ratings as information rather than automatically replacing them by one unquestioned truth label. It does not adjudicate the JudgmentBench labels or identify which judge–human disagreements are model errors.

25. **`borgohain2026disagreement` — Satya Borgohain and Roy Mariathas (2026), _Decomposing Physician Disagreement in HealthBench_.** [Record, v2, 8 March 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22758v2); [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.22758v2), DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2602.22758. Table 1 has 60,896 physician–case ratings over 29,511 prompt–completion–rubric cases. Table 2's 81.8% is residual **label-level variance**, alongside 2.4% physician and 15.8% rubric components. It is not 81.8% of disagreement variance or proven irreducible noise. Table 3's rubric ICCs are 3.6–3.9% on the observed LPM scale and 6.9% on the latent-logit scale, not one confidence interval. Section 3.8 considers prediction/triage utility, but not a fixed-budget AND eager/gated policy experiment. Use both authors' names, not “et al.”

26. **`rao2026agreement` — Delip Rao and Chris Callison-Burch (2026), _Agreement Metrics for LLM-as-Judge Evaluation: What to Report and Why_.** [Record, v2, 31 July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00093v2); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00093v2). The HTML title instead says _Agreement Measurement for Rubric-based LLM Judges: What to Report and Why_; the existing note preserves this discrepancy. The paper studies agreement scale, missingness and aggregation; its reporting guidance distinguishes estimands and recommends resampling the dependence-carrying unit. Section 5.3 also separates identification from sampling uncertainty. “Aggregation matters” and “report the correct unit” therefore cannot stand alone as new claims in our manuscript.

27. **`chen2026partial` — Xiaohong Chen, Ashesh Rambachan and Elie Tamer (2026), _Partial Identification from LLM Prompts_.** [Record, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15031v2); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15031v2). The record dates v2 to 24 June 2026 while the HTML front matter displays 24 August; retain the existing note. The paper studies latent truth under prompt/reporting information, allows dependence, and describes how coarsening can enlarge identified sets. Use it as neighboring information-sufficiency work. Our fixed-reference task-link disclosure changes observable associations at a fixed target; it is not the same latent-truth identification problem. Do not claim that marginal-versus-joint nonidentification itself originates here.

## Citation and wording decisions

- This focused set is sufficient for a compact related-work section; it is not a requirement to place all 27 in the main text. Move method-specific sources to the relevant appendix if their methods only appear there. Preserve the 55-entry reading library rather than deleting unused entries.
- Group the three Krivosheev papers carefully: crowd screening, machine–crowd hybrid screening and classifier-retraining active learning are related but distinct operations.
- State each guarantee beside its assumptions. Logical certification relative to fixed reference bits, probabilistic posterior stopping, calibration guarantees, unbiased estimation, asymptotic intervals and optimization convergence are not interchangeable.
- The positive increment to defend is the measured AND update-completion mechanism, its budget-dependent empirical consequences, the certification/repair decomposition and information needed to resolve those consequences. Existing work rules out broad claims of first selection/update separation, first error-flow accounting or first simultaneous study of testing objectives.
- Bibliographic spelling/version discrepancies above are source discrepancies already documented locally, not newly discovered metadata mistakes. No local or public BibTeX entry was changed during this audit.
