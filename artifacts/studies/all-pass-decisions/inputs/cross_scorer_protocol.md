# Cross-scoring early approvals on SummEval

## Locked comparison

This is a secondary analysis of public records. Before this plan, the previous study had reported majority-label outcomes on both article partitions and model approval rates, and the repeated-lawyer result on JudgmentBench was known. No SummEval individual-rater cross-scoring contrasts were examined for this study before this local protocol was written and hashed. The digest records the specification locally. It is not a third-party preregistration.

The primary comparison uses the existing 70-article partition (1,120 summaries, 16 systems per article), UniEval as the initial judge and G-Eval GPT-4 as the auxiliary judge. UniEval was the unanimous selection in the earlier fixed-threshold, equal-cost comparison. The only primary budget is 50% of available criterion instances. False approvals and false rejections cost one each. The other prespecified displays are the fixed-cutoff judge swap, a grid from 0 to 100% in 10-point increments, and the same measurements on all 100 articles (1,600 summaries, overlapping the primary frame). No threshold fitting or model selection.

## Data and paths

Use the six versioned or byte-hashed inputs in src/external_summeval_frame.py and its existing methods for identities, article split, normalized text joins and scoring. Four dimensions are ordered as coherence, consistency, fluency and relevance. Use the fixed model bits from that study, UniEval >= 0.5 on each dimension and G-Eval means >= [4,4,2.5,4]. Reconcile them against the historical primary bit panels and reconcile the per-dimension majority of the three individual expert bits against their saved reference. Require all keys, 16 distinct systems per article and three complete four-dimensional expert vectors with integer ratings 1–5. A person's dimension passes for ratings >=4 and their summary passes iff all four dimensions pass. Slot positions refer to individual records within summary. Do not infer named or independent raters from positions.

Order summaries lexically by (article ID, system ID), then flatten the four dimensions. For each frame use the existing make_order on observable model bits with policy disagreement_then_random, task order release, and seeds 1 through 31. It checks model disagreements first in output order with seeded within-output ties, then other items in a globally randomized order. Use the first floor(total criteria × integer budget percent / 100) queries. No auditor or final scorer can affect these positions. At a given budget and seed the entire set is identical for all three auditors and both judges. The budget is global, not two dimensions per summary at 50%.

For each output, rotate each of its three expert vectors as auditor. Eager uses the auditor's answer at a checked position, the primary judge elsewhere, and then takes their conjunction. Gated retains the initial verdict until a checked failure certifies FAIL or all dimensions have been checked and passed. Freeze both verdicts before scoring. Evaluate each against its auditor, then separately against each other expert. Give every output equal weight, each auditor weight 1/3, and its two distinct external scorers weight 1/2. Do not use majority labels in loss calculation.

## Outcomes and uncertainty

An early approval is an output that passes under eager and fails under gated at the stated budget. Report its average count per auditor/order, rate per 100 outputs, and the percentage of these cases endorsed by own and other scorers (pooled count ratios rather than means of percentages). Loss difference D is 100 times (gated errors minus eager errors) divided by outputs. Positive favors eager. Report D_own, D_other and their paired difference D_other − D_own. Opposite nonzero signs are a point-estimate reversal. Call a reversal supported by both marginal intervals only when the two pointwise intervals exclude zero in opposite directions. Also report the interval on the paired change. Show underlying per-pair results and per-article totals for reproduction. When zero early approvals occur, their endorsement fraction is undefined.

Use shared 1,999 whole-article composition draws with replacement (NumPy default_rng(20261125).multinomial(n, ones(n)/n, size=1999) on ascending article IDs). For each draw reweight all auditor/scorer roles and the 31 realized orders jointly; divide by the draw's output count. Report equal-tailed percentile 95% bounds, conditional on these paths, recorded experts and systems. Apply the same weighting principle on JudgmentBench, resampling its 30 base tasks. Intervals are pointwise, and there is no joint test across the grid. The output count, not number of pairings or seeds, is the unit denominator.

The prespecified historical comparator is JudgmentBench full GPT-5.4 on its 206 repeatedly scored outputs. Fix each output's archived judge vectors and disagreement-first paths. Cycle all released attorneys on that output in both roles, weighting within output as above for its actual rater count. The old hash-selected A/B comparison is a cross-check. Report both experiments side by side at 50% and label their different rubric lengths, costs in criterion checks, scoring targets and group sizes. A smaller shift or the same preferred rule on SummEval counts against generalizing the legal reversal. The direction of the role-averaged shift for these fixed nonadaptive masks follows from the conjunction; the magnitude and crossing of zero are empirical.
